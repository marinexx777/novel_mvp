title: "— AFTER THE SIRENS"
---
Years later, their daughter would ask why there was a faded piece of paper beside the front door.

By then, the city sounded ordinary again.

Buses hissed at stops. Trains ran late. Pubs filled on Friday nights. Hospital corridors were busy for all the usual reasons. People complained about traffic as if empty roads had never frightened them.

The Ravensbridge Line still existed.

It had fewer messages now. Mostly winter lifts for elderly patients, food-bank deliveries, storm support and the occasional emergency request from St Anne's.

Ethan still drove when he could.

Maya still volunteered at the community centre twice a month.

Arthur survived long enough to meet their daughter and inform Ethan that fatherhood had not made him less boring.

Claire became a consultant. Jamie became a paramedic. Mick finally began driving lessons at fifty and abandoned them after three sessions, claiming the instructor "lacked faith".

The city did not become kinder forever.

People argued again. They forgot things. They complained. Some rewrote the crisis to make themselves braver than they had been.

Ethan did not mind.

He knew what he had seen.

Fear. Anger. Selfishness. Exhaustion.

And beside all of it, ordinary people carrying groceries up stairs, lending cars, sewing masks, answering phones, making soup, driving through snow and waiting outside hospital doors.

One winter evening, his daughter pointed at the old note.

"Daddy, what does that mean?"

COME HOME.

Ethan looked at Maya across the hallway.

She smiled.

"It means," he said, "that when everything gets frightening, you remember where you belong—and you help other people find their way back too."

Outside, Ravensbridge shone beneath Christmas lights.

This time, the streets were full.
