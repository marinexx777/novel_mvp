chapter: 25
title: "— THE COST OF BEING FIRST"
---
Aurora's success attracted attention, money and imitation.

Daniel became a public figure almost by accident.

He disliked most of it.

He liked one thing: people began talking about nuclear medicine as more than a frightening word involving radiation.

He used interviews to explain targeted therapy, imaging, safety and uncertainty in plain language.

"Radioactive is not a diagnosis," he said on television. "Dose, isotope, route and purpose matter. A smoke detector and a house fire both involve smoke. That does not make them the same event."

The clip spread widely.

Helen sent him a message: Not terrible.

From her, it was affection.
