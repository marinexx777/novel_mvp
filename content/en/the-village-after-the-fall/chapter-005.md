chapter: 5
title: "— THE LOAN"
---
Rowan King should have become Martin's enemy.

Instead, three hours after they punched each other, they were sitting in a café discussing money.

Rowan's business had grown from farm distribution into regional food logistics, cold storage and small-brand investment. He understood cash flow better than Martin expected.

"Claire says your Cornish project could restart," Rowan said.

"The council wants it completed. Buyers want homes, not a monument to my stupidity."

"How much?"

"To restart safely? Four hundred thousand. To pretend I can solve everything? A million."

Rowan smiled. "At least bankruptcy improved your arithmetic."

Martin wanted to dislike him. It was becoming inconvenient.

Rowan offered a one-year secured loan of four hundred thousand pounds at a commercial rate. No equity. Strict milestones. Independent solicitor. Funds released in stages against verified work.

The old Martin would have been offended by the controls.

The new Martin found them reassuring.

"Why help me?"

Rowan considered the question.

"Because the project is worth more finished than dead. Because Claire thinks you should have one fair chance to clean up your mess. And because I prefer lending to men who have already discovered they are not immortal."

Martin signed.

Harbour Rise restarted.

This time he did not delegate blindly. Every sales claim went through legal review. Deposits sat in protected accounts. Contractors were paid on verified stages. Martin met former staff who wanted to return and accepted some, but not his old commercial director, who had concealed unlawful sales practices during the first project.

The work was slower than before.

It was also real.

Within eighteen months the development was finished, the final units sold, workers paid, buyers compensated, and Rowan repaid with interest.

Martin could have used the restored reputation to borrow again.

Instead, he closed the company.

At the farewell meeting, his team thought he was joking.

"You survived the worst property collapse of your life," one manager said. "And now you're quitting?"

"Exactly."

He returned to Bristol with far less money than he had once possessed and no debt he could not explain.

He slept eleven hours the first night.

The next morning he woke without an alarm and experienced a sensation so unfamiliar he lay still trying to identify it.

Peace.
