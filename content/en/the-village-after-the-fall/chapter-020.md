chapter: 20
title: "— A VILLAGE IS NOT A BRAND"
---
Martin's first mistake in public office was trying to market Willowbrook before asking what residents wanted it to become.

He proposed a "Willowbrook Heritage Village" identity, visitor signage, an annual craft festival and a destination campaign.

A young mother stood up at a meeting.

"That's lovely. Can the heritage village have a bus that gets my daughter to college?"

An older renter asked about insulation.

A farm worker asked about broadband.

A teenager asked why every plan assumed young people wanted to leave or serve tourists.

Martin went home irritated.

Then ashamed.

He had done it again: built the brochure before understanding the lives inside it.

The next meeting had no presentation.

Only questions.

What makes it hard to stay here?

What makes it worth staying?

What would bring you back?

What should never change?

The answers produced a plan different from Martin's.

A shared minibus. Better broadband. Four affordable rental units in converted barns. Workspace for small businesses. The training centre. A weekly health clinic. Childcare two days a week. Protection for the willow beds and wetland landscape. Visitor income, yes—but not at the price of turning every home into a holiday let.

Martin learned another lesson.

A village was not a brand.

It was a negotiation between memory and need.
