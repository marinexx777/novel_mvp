chapter: 6
title: "— STARTING FROM ZERO"
---
Writing a CV after five years at home felt like drafting evidence for her own disappearance.

Education: strong.

Languages: good French, conversational Spanish, some Japanese learned out of curiosity.

Experience: one year in hotel operations.

Then a white gap large enough to swallow the page.

Full-time parent, she wrote.

She deleted it.

Career break for childcare.

She deleted that too.

Finally she left it in.

For a week, applications vanished into automated systems. The only recruiters who called wanted commission-only salespeople.

Then the Grand Ebor Hotel invited her for an interview.

It was a five-star property near the city centre, privately owned, known for mixing contemporary luxury with Yorkshire history.

The HR manager read Eleanor's CV and was polite enough not to look disappointed.

“We wouldn't place you in management immediately,” she said. “But we have a role in the restaurant guest team.”

“A waitress?”

“Guest service associate.”

Eleanor nearly laughed.

The manager continued. “We have a lot of international visitors. We want staff who can explain regional food and local history, not simply carry plates.”

A man who had been sitting at the side of the room looked up from Eleanor's CV.

He was in his early forties, well dressed without seeming to try, with the calm expression of somebody accustomed to solving problems quietly.

“May I ask something?” he said.

The HR manager straightened. “Of course, Mr Cole.”

Eleanor recognised the name. Adrian Cole, general manager and part-owner.

He asked, “Which matters more to you now—your daughter or your career?”

The question irritated her enough to remove fear.

“They aren't competitors,” she said.

“Sometimes they are.”

“Then the work is to manage the conflict honestly. Five years ago I left my job because I thought being a good mother required me to disappear professionally. I regret that. But I don't regret raising my daughter. If you hire me, I will work hard. I also need predictable daytime shifts for now because she has recently lost her father.”

Adrian studied her.

“That's inconvenient for us.”

“Yes.”

“You're not trying to pretend otherwise.”

“No.”

“What do you bring that makes the inconvenience worthwhile?”

Eleanor sat straighter.

“I know this city. I know hospitality. I can speak to international guests. I can explain why Yorkshire pudding is not dessert, why tourists should stop calling every old lane 'Diagon Alley', where to eat well without queuing for forty minutes, and why a building's story matters to the experience of staying in it.”

The corner of Adrian's mouth moved.

The HR manager looked at him.

He nodded once.

“We'll try it,” he said. “Lunch service. Reduced base hours. Standard service charge and benefits. Three-month probation.”

Eleanor's throat tightened.

“When do I start?”

“Monday.”

Outside the hotel, she stood on the pavement and breathed.

It was not the career she had once imagined.

It was a door.

For now, that was enough.
