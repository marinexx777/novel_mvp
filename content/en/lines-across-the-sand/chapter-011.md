chapter: 11
title: "— THE RUMOUR"
---
Maya’s identity became public after an accident.

She fainted during a machinery inspection and fell against a steel bracket, tearing her shoulder badly enough to require hospital treatment.

The immediate concern was medical. Daniel stopped work, checked her breathing and ordered an ambulance to the nearest private hospital. Toby went with her. Graham stayed on site because the project could not lose its director during an active shift.

He watched the vehicle leave with both hands clenched.

“Go,” Daniel said quietly. “Owen can run the site.”

Graham shook his head. “I’m responsible here.”

“And she’s your daughter.”

“That’s exactly why I can’t pretend the rules are different for me.”

Daniel disliked the answer and respected it at the same time.

Maya required stitches and observation. Her condition was not serious.

The problem began when someone at the site clinic realised Maya was female and mentioned it in a work group.

Within hours the information spread.

By the next morning the story had changed. Some said Graham had secretly placed his daughter in a protected role. Others claimed Daniel had been giving her favourable treatment. A few messages implied a relationship between Daniel and Maya.

Toby stormed into Daniel’s room waving his phone.

“This is ridiculous.”

Daniel read the thread and felt irritation cool into something more analytical.

“It is ridiculous.”

“Exactly.”

“Which means it shouldn’t be spreading this well.”

Toby stopped.

Daniel traced the messages. The most persistent versions came from one work group supervised temporarily by engineer Martin Cole, a competent technician who disliked management responsibility and had recently been criticised for passing problems down to junior staff.

Martin had not invented every rumour, but he had repeated enough of them and added insinuations about “connections” to give the gossip authority.

When confronted, he became defensive.

“I said what people were already saying.”

“You gave it credibility.”

“I’m not responsible for what adults believe.”

Daniel’s voice hardened. “If you supervise people, you are responsible for what you spread among them.”

Graham wanted formal discipline.

Daniel agreed but argued against humiliation.

“The issue isn’t that Maya is a woman. The issue is that she felt she had to hide it to be judged on her work. If we turn this into a spectacle, we prove her point.”

Maya returned two days later with her arm in a sling and walked into the engineering briefing without scarf, cap or disguise.

Nobody spoke.

She looked around the room.

“I’m Maya Bennett. I’m a structural engineer. Those two facts have always been true. If anyone has a technical concern about my work, bring it to me. If the concern is that I’m a woman, I can’t fix your problem.”

Toby began clapping.

Daniel joined him.

Then everyone did.

Graham looked down at his notes so nobody would see his face.
