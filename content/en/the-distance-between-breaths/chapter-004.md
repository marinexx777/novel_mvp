---
chapter: 4
title: "— THE LIFE SHE WOULD NOT QUIT"
---
Ben Carter could not understand why Lena refused a better life.

From his perspective, this was not selfishness. It was arithmetic.

His family owned a development company, several hotels, and enough property in British Columbia that no one in the Carter household had ever checked a bank balance before ordering dinner. Lena earned a respectable physician’s salary but worked nights, weekends, holidays, and occasionally twenty hours without seeing daylight.

Why?

When Ben asked, Lena said, “Because patients don’t schedule respiratory failure around brunch.”

He found the answer infuriating.

His parents found it worse.

They liked Lena’s education, composure, and beauty. They did not like the hospital calls, the infection risk, or the fact that she had no desire to structure her career around Ben’s future.

“You could run a private clinic,” his mother had suggested. “Something elegant. Executive health.”

Lena had smiled politely and changed the subject.

The argument sharpened when Ben announced a birthday party in a private club.

“Cancel it,” Lena said.

“Why?”

“The pneumonia cases are increasing.”

“You’ve been saying that for two weeks.”

“Because they are.”

“It’s a birthday party, not a bioterrorism convention.”

“Crowded room, bad ventilation, people drinking and shouting for hours. If one person is infected—”

Ben groaned.

“Do you hear yourself?”

“Yes. That’s why I’m asking you not to do it.”

“You’re afraid of everything now.”

“No. I’m afraid of one specific thing for specific reasons.”

He held the silence for several seconds before delivering the sentence that ended more than he realised.

“Then quit.”

Lena stared at the phone.

“Come west with me. We get married. You work when you want. We have children before we’re forty. You stop giving every piece of yourself to strangers who don’t even know your name.”

He meant it as love.

She heard erasure.

“I have to go,” she said.

That night she performed CPR on a patient whose oxygen saturation had collapsed without warning.

The patient died before dawn.

It was the hospital’s first death from the unexplained pneumonia.

Lena stood in the changing room afterward, drenched in sweat beneath her protective equipment, staring at the floor as if it might tell her what she had missed.

Adrian found her there.

“You did everything right,” he said.

“That doesn’t make him less dead.”

“No.”

“There are twelve more in isolation.”

“Yes.”

“What if they all go like that?”

Adrian pulled a carton of warm milk from a paper bag and set it beside her.

She looked at him.

“You remember?”

“I remember everything useful.”

“Convenient definition.”

“Drink.”

They sat in silence.

Then Lena’s phone buzzed.

A photograph appeared from an unknown number.

Ben, shirtless, asleep in a hotel bed beside a woman Lena recognised from his social circle.

Lena stared at the image for a long time.

Adrian saw only her face.

“Bad news?”

She locked the screen.

“No,” she said, surprised by how calm she sounded. “Just clarity.”
