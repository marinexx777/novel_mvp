---
chapter: 22
title: "— MARTIN AND HELEN"
---
Martin Ward returned to Lakeshore as part of a national clinical advisory group.

Helen found out from television.

She called him for the first time in eleven years.

“You went to Lakeshore and didn’t tell me.”

Martin nearly dropped his phone.

“Helen?”

“Who else would call to tell you you’re an idiot?”

He sat down slowly.

“You haven’t changed.”

“Neither have you, apparently. Our daughter is in a fever clinic and you manage to communicate with her through your former student.”

“I messaged her.”

“Oh, congratulations. Father of the year.”

The old anger was still there, but age had taken some poison out of it.

Martin did not defend himself.

“I failed her,” he said.

Helen stopped.

That was not how their arguments used to go.

He told her about the slap. About the accusation involving Rebecca Shaw. About how, even after he understood Lena’s fear, pride had prevented him from giving her the one thing she needed: a patient explanation.

Helen was silent.

“I didn’t help,” she admitted. “I let my anger at you become part of her childhood.”

Martin closed his eyes.

For years they had each told themselves the other parent had created the distance.

Now the outbreak had made distance literal enough to expose the emotional version.

When Martin entered quarantine housing after a hospital visit, meals began arriving at reception.

Vegetable stew. Rice. Braised beef. Soup.

No sender.

He recognized Helen’s cooking anyway.

He called her.

“This is too much food.”

“You always forget to eat when you work.”

“You remember?”

“I remember everything annoying.”

He smiled.

“So does Lena.”

They did not speak about reconciliation.

They began with dinner.
