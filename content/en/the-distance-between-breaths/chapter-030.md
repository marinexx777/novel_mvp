chapter: 30
title: "— THE DISTANCE BETWEEN BREATHS"
---
One year later, Lena and Adrian were still at Lakeshore University Hospital.

They were not married.

Not yet.

This annoyed Helen, amused Rachel, and relieved Lena, who had discovered that rebuilding a relationship in adulthood was less dramatic and more complicated than falling in love at twenty.

They argued about schedules.

They negotiated whose apartment contained too many medical journals.

They learned that Adrian left cupboard doors open and Lena became silent when truly angry.

They also learned to ask questions before inventing answers.

Peter stepped down as department head and remained on staff. Rachel moved back to Lakeshore permanently and began teaching emergency preparedness while continuing part-time clinical work.

Daniel returned to cardiology slowly. Olivia returned to respiratory nursing after an extended leave.

Martin and Helen did not formally reunite, at least not in any way their daughter could define. They began having dinner together whenever Martin visited Kingston. Helen occasionally travelled to Toronto. Neither discussed what the arrangement meant.

Lena decided not to demand a label.

Some repairs required less inspection.

On the anniversary of the city shutdown, Lena finished a late shift and found Adrian waiting outside the hospital with two coffees.

“No warm milk?” she asked.

“I thought we had evolved.”

“Disappointing.”

They walked toward the lake.

At the pedestrian bridge, Adrian stopped.

“I have something for you.”

Lena narrowed her eyes.

“If this is a ring in public, I’m walking away.”

“It is not a ring.”

He handed her a small metal plaque.

She read it.

FOR SERVICES TO ADOLESCENT EMOTIONAL STABILITY DURING SARS
ADRIAN SHAW, DOORWAY CONSULTANT

Lena laughed so hard she had to lean against the railing.

“You actually had this made.”

“I believe in recognizing excellence.”

She wiped tears from her eyes.

Then Adrian became serious.

“Lena.”

She looked at him.

“I don’t need an answer tonight. But I want the next part of my life to be ordinary with you.”

“Ordinary?”

“Groceries. Bad shifts. Holidays we miss. Arguments we finish. Parents who get older. Maybe children, maybe not. Travel when the world allows it. Illness when it doesn’t. I’m not asking for a perfect life.”

His voice softened.

“I’m asking for a shared one.”

Lena thought of all the years they had lost to silence, all the people lost to a disease no one understood until understanding came too late for some of them.

She thought of breath: invisible, automatic, ignored until it became difficult.

Love could be like that too.

Not the grand declaration.

The ongoing exchange.

In and out.

Given and received.

“I want that,” she said.

Adrian smiled.

“Good.”

“Was that a proposal?”

“Absolutely not. You threatened to leave.”

“Smart man.”

They continued across the bridge.

Below them, traffic moved through Lakeshore again. Trains crossed the river. Restaurant lights shone along the waterfront. Somewhere behind them, an ambulance entered the hospital drive and another team prepared to meet it.

Life had not returned to what it was before.

That was never how survival worked.

It had become something else: scarred, unfinished, ordinary, precious.

Lena took Adrian’s hand.

Between one breath and the next, the city kept living.
